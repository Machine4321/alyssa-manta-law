⚡ MODULE I: EFFECT AND APPLICATION OF LAWS & CONFLICT OF LAWS (NCC Arts. 1–18)

1. Effectivity of Laws (Art. 2 NCC & *Tañada v. Tuvera*)

• ⚡ 5-Second Rule: Publication is indispensable; laws take effect after 15 days following completion of publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation, unless the law itself provides a different period (e.g., "immediately upon publication" or "30 days after publication").
• "Unless it is otherwise provided" refers only to the 15-day period, NEVER to the requirement of publication itself. Even if a law says "This Act shall take effect immediately upon approval," it still requires publication first.

⚡ MODULE II & III: PERSONS & FAMILY RELATIONS (Family Code, R.A. 11596, R.A. 11642)

1. Juridical Capacity vs. Capacity to Act (Arts. 37–39 NCC)

• Juridical Capacity: Fitness to be the subject of legal relations; inherent in every natural person from birth (and provisional personality from conception for all purposes favorable to the child under Art. 40 NCC) and lost only through death.
• Capacity to Act: Power to do acts with legal effect; acquired at age 18 (R.A. 6809) and restricted by minority, insanity, imbecility, prodigality, or civil interdiction.
• R.A. No. 11596 (Prohibition of Child Marriage Act): Facilitation and solemnization of a marriage where one or both parties are under 18 years old is a public crime; child marriage is void ab initio.

2. Essential vs. Formal Requisites of Marriage (Arts. 2–4 FC)

• Essential Requisites (Art. 2): (1) Legal capacity of the contracting parties who must be a male and a female; (2) Consent freely given in the presence of the solemnizing officer.
• Formal Requisites (Art. 3): (1) Authority of the solemnizing officer; (2) A valid marriage license (except in Arts. 27–34 FC); (3) A marriage ceremony with personal appearance of the parties and at least two witnesses of legal age.
• Effect of Absence, Defect, or Irregularity (Art. 4 FC):
• Absence of any essential or formal requisite $\rightarrow$ VOID AB INITIO (Exception: Art. 35[2] FC — where the solemnizing officer lacked authority, the marriage is STILL VALID if either or both parties believed in good faith that the solemnizing officer had the legal authority to do so — mistake of fact, not mistake of law!).
• Defect in an essential requisite (vitiated consent under Art. 45 FC) $\rightarrow$ VOIDABLE.
• Irregularity in a formal requisite (e.g., marriage license issued without waiting 10 days, or only 1 witness, or marriage held outside chambers without written request) $\rightarrow$ VALID, but subjects the responsible party to civil/criminal/administrative liability.

3. Marriages Exempt from a Marriage License (Arts. 27–34 FC)

• Art. 34 FC (5-Year Cohabitation as Husband and Wife):

1. The man and woman must have lived together as husband and wife for at least 5 years prior to the marriage; AND

2. They must have no legal impediment to marry each other at the time of the actual marriage ceremony (Borja-Manzano v. Sanchez; note that under Niñal v. Bayadog vs. Manzano, the 5-year period is counted immediately preceding the day of the marriage).

4. Psychological Incapacity (Art. 36 FC — *Tan-Andal v. Andal*, G.R. No. 196359, May 11, 2021 En Banc)

• ⚡ 5-Second Rule: Psychological incapacity is a LEGAL concept, NOT a medical illness! No psychologist/psychiatrist testimony is mandatory.
• Key Tan-Andal Doctrines (Timoteo Aquino 3rd Ed. Highlight):

1. Quantum of Proof: Must be proven by clear and convincing evidence (higher than preponderance, lower than proof beyond reasonable doubt).

2. Three Requisites:

• Juridical Antecedence: Must be rooted in the history/personality structure of the party antedating the marriage, even if overt manifestations appear only during the marriage.
• Gravity: Must be caused by a genuinely serious psychic cause (not mere "difficulty," "refusal," or "neglect"), consisting of an enduring aspect of personality structure that makes it impossible for the spouse to understand and comply with essential marital obligations.
• Incurability: Now understood in the legal sense (not medical)—meaning the incapacity is so enduring and persistent with respect to the specific partner (relative incurability) that the marriage will inevitably break down.

5. Mixed Marriages & Foreign Divorce (Art. 26[2] FC)

• Rule (Republic v. Manalo, G.R. No. 221029, April 24, 2018 En Banc): Where a marriage between a Filipino citizen and a foreigner is validly celebrated and a divorce is thereafter validly obtained abroad by EITHER the alien spouse OR the Filipino spouse capacitating them to remarry, the Filipino spouse shall likewise have capacity to remarry under Philippine law.
• 2026 Bar Exam Traps on Foreign Divorce:

1. Both Filipino at the Time of Divorce (2026 Bar Q2): If a Filipino wife obtains a divorce in Nevada while both she and her husband are still Filipino citizens, the divorce is VOID and not recognized in the Philippines (Art. 15 NCC). Even if the husband later marries an American citizen in the US, that second marriage is VOID for bigamy!

2. Divorce by Mutual Agreement in Japan (2026 Bar Q4 — Racho v. Tanaka / Galapon v. Republic): A foreign divorce obtained by mutual agreement between a Filipino and a Japanese spouse is VALID and recognizable under Art. 26(2) FC! It is NOT collusion under Philippine law as long as it is valid under the national law of the foreign spouse.

3. Proof Required: The Filipino spouse must file a petition for judicial recognition of foreign judgment and prove both (a) the fact of divorce AND (b) the national law of the foreign spouse as official acts of a foreign sovereign under Rule 132, Secs. 24–25.

6. Property Relations of Spouses & Unmarried Partners

• Default Regime:
• Marriages before Aug. 3, 1988 $\rightarrow$ Conjugal Partnership of Gains (CPG).
• Marriages on or after Aug. 3, 1988 $\rightarrow$ Absolute Community of Property (ACP).
• Sale or Encumbrance Without Spousal Consent (Arts. 96 & 124 FC): Any disposition or encumbrance of community/conjugal property without the written consent of the other spouse (or court authority) is VOID, and operates only as a continuing offer until accepted/consented to before withdrawal.
• Waiver of Share During Marriage (Arts. 89 & 107 FC — 2026 Bar Q1): No waiver of rights, interests, shares, and effects of the ACP or CPG can be made during the marriage except in case of judicial separation of property! A husband's waiver of his conjugal share in favor of his illegitimate child during the marriage is VOID ab initio, so the child's subsequent sale to a buyer transfers nothing (nemo dat quod non habet).
• Art. 147 vs. Art. 148 FC (Co-Ownership in Void Marriages / Cohabitation):
• Art. 147 (Capacitated to Marry Each Other — e.g., Art. 36 Nullity or No License): Wages and salaries are owned in equal shares; properties acquired while living together are presumed obtained through joint efforts, and a partner who did not participate in acquisition is deemed to have contributed jointly if their efforts consisted in the care and maintenance of the family and household.
• Art. 148 (Incapacitated to Marry Each Other — e.g., Bigamous Marriage or Adulterous Relationship): Only the properties acquired through their ACTUAL joint contribution of money, property, or industry shall be owned in common in proportion to their respective contributions. Domestic/household care alone does NOT give a share under Art. 148!

7. Domestic Administrative Adoption (R.A. No. 11642)

• Domestic adoption in the Philippines is now purely administrative under the National Authority for Child Care (NACC) via an Order of Adoption (no longer judicial!).
• Effects: The adopted child is considered a legitimate child of the adopter for all intents and purposes, and all legal ties between the biological parents and the adoptee are severed (except when the biological parent is the spouse of the adopter).

⚡ MODULE V & VI: PROPERTY, OWNERSHIP & LAND TITLES AND DEEDS

1. Builder in Good Faith (Art. 448 NCC) vs. Lessee (Art. 1678 NCC) — *2026 Bar Q15*

• ⚡ 5-Second Rule: A lessee is NEVER a builder in good faith under Art. 448!
• Art. 448 (Builder in Good Faith — believes he owns the land):
• The Landowner has two options:

1. Appropriate the building after paying indemnity under Arts. 546 & 548 (necessary + useful expenses), during which the builder has the right of retention (without paying rent!); OR

2. Compel the builder to buy the land, UNLESS the value of the land is considerably more than the value of the building (in which case the builder cannot be forced to buy, and they must agree on a forced lease).

• Art. 1678 (Lessee Who Builds Useful Improvements with Lessor's Permission — 2026 Bar Q15):
• Because a lessee knows from day one that he is merely renting, Art. 448 never applies.
• Under Art. 1678 NCC, upon termination of the lease, the Lessor has the option to pay only ONE-HALF (1/2) of the value of the useful improvements at that time.
• If the lessor refuses to reimburse $1/2$, the lessee's only right is to remove the improvements (even if it causes damage to the leased premises, provided he causes no more impairment than is necessary). A lessee has NO right of retention!

2. River Branching vs. Island Formation (Arts. 463–465 NCC) — *2026 Bar Q8*

• Art. 463 (Current Divides and Isolates an Intact Piece of Land): Whenever the current of a river divides itself into branches, leaving a piece of land or part thereof isolated, the owner of the land retains his ownership (applies only when an existing intact portion of the land is cut off by a new river branch, NOT when soil is gradually eroded and accumulates into a new island!).
• Arts. 464 & 465 (Islands Formed by Successive Accumulation of Dragged-Down Superior Alluviums):
• Art. 464: Islands formed in the seas within the maritime zone, or in navigable or floatable rivers, belong to the State as property of public dominion (Art. 502[4] NCC) and cannot be acquired by prescription.
• Art. 465: Islands formed through successive accumulation in non-navigable and non-floatable rivers belong to the owners of the margins or banks nearest to each of them (or divided longitudinally in halves if in the middle of the river). The upstream owner whose soil was gradually eroded loses ownership of the eroded soil.

3. Land Titles & Deeds (P.D. No. 1529 & R.A. No. 11573)

• R.A. No. 11573 (Judicial Confirmation of Imperfect Title — Republic v. Pasig Rizal Co., Inc., Feb. 15, 2022 En Banc):

1. Shortened the required possession period under Sec. 14(1) of P.D. 1529 from "since June 12, 1945" to at least twenty (20) years immediately preceding the filing of the application for confirmation of title!

2. Deleted Sec. 14(2) (acquisitive prescription of patrimonial land), clarifying that for agricultural lands of the public domain, the applicant only needs to prove: (a) the land is classified as Alienable and Disposable (A&D) at the time of filing (proven by a certification of the DENR geodetic engineer with the relevant DAO/Proclamation number imprinted on the survey plan), and (b) 20 years of open, continuous, exclusive, and notorious possession under a bona fide claim of ownership.

• Forged Deed & Innocent Purchaser for Value (Sec. 53, P.D. 1529):
• General Rule: A forged deed is a nullity and conveys no title.
• Exception (Chain of Title Doctrine): A forged deed may become the root of a valid title ONLY when the certificate of title has already been transferred from the name of the true owner to the name of the forger (or fictitious person), and while it stands in the forger's name, an Innocent Purchaser for Value (IPV) buys it relying in good faith on the face of the Torrens title.
• Assurance Fund & Notice of Lis Pendens (Sec. 95, P.D. 1529 — 2026 Bar Q10):
• To recover from the Assurance Fund, the claimant must be in good faith and without negligence, and must be barred from recovering the land itself.
• A buyer who purchases land after a Notice of Lis Pendens has already been annotated on the seller's title is a transferee pendente lite with constructive notice of the defect; he is NOT in good faith and cannot recover from the Assurance Fund!

⚡ MODULE VIII & IX: OBLIGATIONS, CONTRACTS & SPECIAL CONTRACTS

1. Joint Indivisible Obligations & Delay (Arts. 1165, 1209, 1224 NCC) — *2026 Bar Q11*

• ⚡ 5-Second Rule: If two debtors bind themselves to deliver a specific car without stipulating solidarity, the obligation is JOINT INDIVISIBLE (Art. 1209).
• What happens if the creditor demands delivery from Debtor A only, and then a fortuitous event (lightning) destroys the car while Debtor B is delivering it?

1. Because the obligation is joint, demand on Debtor A puts Debtor A in delay (mora solvendi), and under Art. 1165(3) NCC, if the obligor delays, he is responsible for any fortuitous event until he has effected the delivery!

2. Under Art. 1224 NCC, a joint indivisible obligation that becomes impossible to perform is converted into an obligation to indemnify for damages:

• Both debtors (Tony and Jojo) remain liable for their respective proportionate share of the value/price of the car;
• Only the debtor in delay/fault (Tony) is liable for additional damages!

• 🔥 2026 Bar Q7 Trap (Sale Between Two 16-Year-Old Minors):
• When BOTH buyer and seller are minors (16 years old), the contract is UNENFORCEABLE under Art. 1403(3) NCC (NOT void and NOT voidable!).
• Under Art. 1407 NCC, if the parent/guardian of one minor ratifies the contract, it transforms into a voidable contract; if the parents/guardians of both minors ratify it, it becomes valid from inception.
• 🔥 2026 Bar Q12 Trap (Oral 5-Year Lease Where Lessee Took Possession):
• While a lease of real property for more than 1 year falls under the Statute of Frauds (Art. 1403[2][e]), the Statute of Frauds applies ONLY to purely executory contracts!
• Once the lessee takes possession and pays rent (partial performance / acceptance of benefits under Art. 1405 NCC), the oral contract is ratified and fully enforceable in an action for breach of contract!

3. Relativity of Contracts, Expired Notary, & Delivery (Arts. 1311, 1358, 1524 NCC) — *2026 Bar Q14*

• Expired Notarial Commission (Art. 1358 NCC): A Deed of Absolute Sale of land notarized by a lawyer whose notarial commission had already expired is merely converted into a private instrument, NOT a void sale! Under Arts. 1356 & 1458 NCC, a contract of sale of real property is consensual and valid between the parties even if in a private instrument (notarization is only for convenience and registrability).
• Heirs Bound by Decedent's Contracts (Art. 1311 NCC): Under the Principle of Relativity, contracts take effect between the parties, their assigns, and heirs (unless the rights/obligations are intransmissible by nature, stipulation, or law). A sole heir is not a stranger to his deceased mother's contract of sale and is bound to deliver the land!
• When Vendor Must Deliver Even Before Price is Paid (Art. 1524 NCC): While the vendor is generally not bound to deliver the thing sold if the vendee has not paid the price, the statutory exception under Art. 1524 is when a period for the payment has been fixed in the contract (unless the vendee has lost the right to make use of the period under Art. 1198).

4. Recto Law (Art. 1484 NCC) vs. Maceda Law (R.A. No. 6552)

• Recto Law (Art. 1484 NCC — Sale of PERSONAL Property on Installments):
• Three (3) Alternative & Mutually Exclusive Remedies:

1. Exact fulfillment (Specific Performance) should the vendee fail to pay any installment;

2. Cancel the sale should the vendee's failure to pay cover two or more installments;

3. Foreclose the chattel mortgage should the vendee's failure to pay cover two or more installments — in this case, he shall have NO further action against the purchaser to recover any unpaid balance of the price, and any agreement to the contrary is void.

• 🔥 2026 Bar Q13 Trap (Execution Sale under Remedy #1 vs. Foreclosure under Remedy #3):
• If the seller chooses Remedy #1 (Specific Performance / Collection of Sum of Money), obtains a money judgment, and the sheriff levies upon and sells the mortgaged vehicle on execution, that is NOT a foreclosure of the chattel mortgage under Art. 1484(3)! Therefore, the seller CAN still recover the deficiency from the buyer!
• Maceda Law (R.A. No. 6552 — Sale of RESIDENTIAL REALTY on Installments):
• If Buyer Paid AT LEAST 2 YEARS of Installments (Sec. 3):

1. Grace Period: 1 month per year of installments paid (exercisable once every 5 years without additional interest).

2. Cash Surrender Value (CSV): Minimum 50% of total payments made (including downpayment/options), plus 5% per year after the 5th year, up to a maximum of 90%.

3. Cancellation Requisites: Valid only 30 days after BOTH (a) receipt of a notarized notice of cancellation AND (b) actual payment of the full CSV!

• If Buyer Paid LESS THAN 2 YEARS of Installments (Sec. 4):

1. Grace Period: Not less than 60 days from the date the installment became due.

2. Cash Surrender Value: None (0%).

⚡ MODULE XI & XII: TORTS, QUASI-DELICTS & DAMAGES

1. Strict Liability of Manufacturers of Food & Drinks (Art. 2187 NCC) — *2026 Bar Q16*

• Art. 2187 NCC: Manufacturers and processors of foodstuffs, drinks, toilet articles, and similar goods shall be liable for death or injuries caused by any noxious or harmful substances used, although no contractual relation exists between them and the consumers.
• Even if a consumer who finds a piece of human bone in a sealed soda bottle suffers no medical illness (2026 Bar Q16), the manufacturer is still liable for Nominal Damages (Art. 2221 NCC) for the technical violation of the consumer's right to safe food/drink, or Temperate/Exemplary Damages for gross negligence in bottling!

2. Quasi-Delict, Self-Defense / Ducking, & Proximate Cause (Art. 2176 NCC) — *2026 Bar Q17*

• When an aggressor (Joma) violently throws a heavy vase at a victim (Mark), and Mark ducks to avoid being hit, causing the vase to fly through the window and injure a pedestrian on the street below:
• Mark is NOT liable because (a) under Art. 2176 NCC, liability for quasi-delict requires fault or negligence, and ducking an unlawful physical assault is a lawful instinct of self-defense / state of necessity without fault; and (b) Joma's intentional act of throwing the vase is the sole proximate cause of the pedestrian's injury!

> Prepared for: Alyssa (Juris Doctor — Wills and Succession Exam Preparation)

> Syllabus Alignment: Supreme Court 2026 Bar Examinations Syllabus — VII. Succession (Civil Code, Arts. 774–1105) & Remedial Law VI.A. Settlement of Estate (Rules 73–90)

> Jurisprudence Cut-Off: Up to June 30, 2025 (per Bar Bulletin No. 1, s. 2026, Associate Justice Samuel H. Gaerlan)

I. GENERAL PROVISIONS & ELEMENTS OF SUCCESSION (Arts. 774–782)

A. Definition and Nature (Arts. 774, 712)

• Succession (Art. 774): A mode of acquisition by virtue of which the property, rights, and obligations to the extent of the value of the inheritance, of a person are transmitted through his death to another or others either by his will or by operation of law.
• Not a Separate Juridical Person, Derived Mode of Acquisition (Art. 712): Succession is a derivative mode of acquiring ownership (ownership is transferred from the decedent to the heirs).

B. Elements of Succession

1. Causal Element (Death of the Decedent — Arts. 775, 777):

• Moment of Transmission (Art. 777): The rights to the succession are transmitted from the moment of the death of the decedent.
• Reckoning Point in Presumptive Death (Aquino Reviewer Core Distinction):
• Ordinary Absence (Art. 390): Absentee disappears under normal circumstances (no danger of death). Presumed dead for purposes of opening succession after 10 years (or 5 years if he disappeared after age 75). Reckoning point of death: At the END of the 10-year or 5-year period.
• Extraordinary / Qualified Absence (Art. 391): Absentee disappears under danger of death: (1) on board a vessel lost during a sea voyage or an aeroplane that is missing; (2) person in the armed forces who has taken part in war; or (3) person who has been in danger of death under other circumstances and his existence has not been known. Presumed dead for all purposes including opening of succession after 4 years. Reckoning point of death: At the BEGINNING of the 4-year period (i.e., the exact date of the calamity or disappearance!).
• 2026 Bar Exam Application (2026 Bar Q#19): Because death under Art. 391 is reckoned on the date the ship sank, any relative who was not yet conceived on the date the ship sank lacks capacity to succeed under Art. 1025, even if born before the petition for settlement is filed 4 years later!
• Landmark Doctrine — Treyes v. Larlar (G.R. No. 232579, Sept. 8, 2020, En Banc):
• By virtue of Art. 777, the rights of the heirs to the inheritance vest immediately at the precise moment of the decedent's death. Heirs do not need a prior judicial declaration of heirship in a separate special proceeding before they can assert their successional rights, file an action to protect/recover hereditary property, or annul simulated/forged deeds in an ordinary civil action (unless there is already a pending special proceeding for the settlement of the estate).

2. Objective Element (The Inheritance — Arts. 776, 781):

• Includes all the property, rights, and obligations of a person which are not extinguished by his death (Art. 776), as well as all that accrues thereto from the opening of the succession (Art. 781).
• Rules on Transmissibility (Aquino's 3 General Rules):

1. Purely personal rights (intuitu personae) are extinguished by death (e.g., marital rights, parental authority, support, usufruct [unless constituted for a fixed period that overrides death], agency, criminal liability).

2. Patrimonial rights and obligations (rights over property and obligations arising from contracts, Art. 1311) are generally transmissible and bind the heirs, unless made intransmissible by nature, stipulation, or law. (Tested in 2026 Bar Q#14: A sole heir is NOT a stranger to a contract of sale executed by his deceased mother and is bound to deliver the land under Art. 1311!)

3. Monetary debts of the decedent are paid by the estate (Rule 86/88, Rules of Court) before distribution; heirs are never personally liable beyond the value of the inheritance they receive (Art. 774, 1311).

• Future Inheritance (Art. 1347): No contract may be entered into upon future inheritance except in cases expressly authorized by law (e.g., partition inter vivos by the testator under Art. 1080). Any waiver or sale of hereditary rights before the decedent's death is void. Once the decedent dies, an heir may validly sell, waive, or assign his hereditary share even before actual partition.

3. Subjective Element (The Successors — Art. 782):

• Heir: A person called to the succession either by the provision of a will (voluntary/compulsory heir) or by operation of law (legal/intestate heir), succeeding by universal title (to the whole or an aliquot/fractional part of the estate).
• Devisee: Person to whom a gift of real property is specifically given by virtue of a will (particular title).
• Legatee: Person to whom a gift of personal property is specifically given by virtue of a will (particular title).
• Importance of Distinction: In preterition (Art. 854), the institution of heirs is annulled in toto, whereas devises and legacies remain valid insofar as they are not inofficious (do not impair the legitime).

4. Acceptance (Arts. 1041–1057):

• Acceptance and repudiation of the inheritance are purely voluntary and free acts (Art. 1041), and their effects always retroact to the moment of death of the decedent (Art. 1042).

II. TESTAMENTARY SUCCESSION (Arts. 783–959)

A. Wills in General (Arts. 783–795)

• Definition (Art. 783): An act whereby a person is permitted, with the formalities prescribed by law, to control to a certain degree the disposition of his estate, to take effect after his death.
• Core Characteristics of a Will:

1. Strictly Personal Act (Non-Delegable — Arts. 784–787):

• Cannot be left in whole or in part to the discretion of a third person, nor accomplished through the instrumentality of an agent or attorney (Art. 784).
• Prohibited Delegations (Art. 785): Duration or efficacy of the designation of heirs, devisees, or legatees, or the determination of the portions which they are to take, when referred to by name, cannot be left to the discretion of a third person.
• Permissible Delegation (Art. 786 — Distribution to a Class): The testator may entrust to a third person the distribution of specific property or sums of money that he may leave in general to specified classes or causes (e.g., "the poor of Manila"), and also the designation of the persons, institutions, or establishments to which such property or sums are to be given or applied.
• Art. 787: The testator may not make a testamentary disposition in such manner that another person has to determine whether or not it is to be operative.

2. Unilateral and Individual Act: Joint wills are prohibited (Art. 818).

3. Free and Voluntary Act: Vitiated by fraud, violence, intimidation, or undue influence (Art. 839).

4. Formal and Solemn Act: Formalities are mandatory (Arts. 804–814).

5. Act Mortis Causa: Produces effect only upon death (Art. 777, 783).

6. Ambulatory and Essentially Revocable (Art. 828): May be revoked at any time before death; any waiver or restriction of the right to revoke is void.

• Rules of Interpretation of Wills (Arts. 788–794):
• Testacy is favored over intestacy (ut res magis valeat quam pereat — Art. 788, 791).
• Latent vs. Patent Ambiguity (Art. 789): Imperfect description or error in name/property may be corrected by extrinsic evidence (aliunde), excluding the oral declarations of the testator as to his intention!
• After-Acquired Property (Art. 793): Property acquired after the making of a will shall only pass thereby, as if the testator had possessed it at the time of making the will, should it expressly appear by the will that such was his intention. (Exception: If the institution is universal or covers the entire estate/aliquot share under Art. 774/781, it covers property existing at death).
• Law Governing Validity of Wills (Time & Place — Arts. 795, 16, 17, 815–819):
Aspect of Validity Governing Law (Time) Governing Law (Place / Conflict of Laws)
Extrinsic / Formal Validity Law in force at the time the will was executed (Art. 795). Legislature cannot validate a void will or invalidate a valid will retroactively. Arts. 17, 815–817: (1) Lex Loci Celebrationis (place of execution); (2) Lex Domicilii (domicile of testator); (3) Lex Nationalii (nationality of testator); or (4) Philippine law. Exception: Joint wills executed by Filipinos are VOID even if executed abroad where allowed (Arts. 818–819).
Intrinsic / Substantive Validity (Order of succession, amount of successional rights/legitimes, intrinsic validity of provisions) Law in force at the time of the decedent's death (Art. 2263, Art. 777). Strictly Lex Nationalii (National Law of the Decedent — Art. 16, par. 2, Art. 1039), regardless of the nature of the property and the country where said property may be found (Bellis v. Bellis, Miciano v. Brimo).

B. Testamentary Capacity and Intent (Arts. 796–803)

• Who May Make a Will: All persons who are not expressly prohibited by law (Art. 796) and who meet two (2) requisites:

1. Age: At least 18 years old at the time of execution (Art. 797); AND

2. Soundness of Mind: At the time of making the will (Art. 798).

• Meaning of Soundness of Mind (Art. 799): It is not necessary that the testator be in full possession of all his reasoning faculties. It is sufficient if he was able at the time of making the will to know:

1. The nature of the estate to be disposed of;

2. The proper objects of his bounty (his relatives/heirs); and

3. The character of the testamentary act.

• Presumption of Sanity (Art. 800): The law presumes that every person is of sound mind, EXCEPT in two instances where the burden shifts to the proponent to prove the will was made during a lucid interval:

1. When the testator, one month, or less, before making his will was publicly known to be insane; OR

2. When the testator made the will after he had been judicially declared insane and before such judicial order has been set aside (Torres v. Lopez).

• Supervening Incapacity vs. Supervening Capacity (Art. 801): Capacity is determined solely at the time of making the will. Supervening incapacity does not invalidate an effective will, nor is the will of an incapable validated by the supervening of capacity.

C. Forms of Wills: Notarial vs. Holographic (Arts. 804–819)

#### 1. Common Requirements for ALL Wills (Art. 804)

1. Must be in writing (nuncupative/oral wills are NOT recognized under the Civil Code); AND

2. Executed in a language or dialect known to the testator (need not be known to the attesting witnesses in a notarial will, Art. 805; knowledge of the testator may be presumed from residence/circumstances or proven by extrinsic evidence, Abangan v. Abangan, Lopez v. Liboro).

#### 2. Formalities of a Notarial (Ordinary) Will (Arts. 805–809)

1. Subscribed at the end thereof by the testator himself OR by the testator's name written by some other person in his presence, and by his express direction (Barut v. Cabacungan: agent must write the testator's name, though it is advisable to add "by Y"; if the agent writes only his own name without the testator's name, the will is void).

2. Attested and subscribed by three (3) or more credible witnesses in the presence of the testator and of one another.

• Test of "In the Presence": Not whether they actually saw each other sign, but whether they could have seen each other sign had they chosen to look, without any physical obstruction (Jaboneta v. Gustilo; contrast with Nera v. Rimando where a curtain obstructed the view).

3. Marginal Signatures: The testator (or the person requested by him to write his name) and the instrumental witnesses shall also sign, as aforesaid, each and every page thereof, except the last, on the left margin (location on right/top/bottom margin is non-fatal, Nayve v. Mojal, Avera v. Garcia; failure of testator or a witness to sign a page is generally fatal unless all pages are signed at the bottom or the omitted page contains no disposition, Icasiano v. Icasiano).

4. Page Numbering: All the pages shall be numbered correlatively in letters placed on the upper part of each page (Arabic numerals `1, 2, 3` or `A, B, C` are valid substantial compliance).

5. Attestation Clause (Mandatory Contents under Art. 805, par. 3):

Must state:

• (a) The number of pages used upon which the will is written;
• (b) The fact that the testator signed the will and every page thereof (or caused some other person to write his name under his express direction) in the presence of the instrumental witnesses; AND
• (c) That the witnesses witnessed and signed the will and all the pages thereof in the presence of the testator and of one another.
• Crucial Distinctions on the Attestation Clause (Art. 809 Doctrine of Liberal Interpretation / Substantial Compliance):
• Omission of Number of Pages in the Attestation Clause: Curable under Art. 809 ONLY if the total number of pages is stated elsewhere on the face of the will itself (e.g., in the notarial acknowledgment, Taboada v. Rosal, Tanchanco v. Santos). If nowhere stated on the face of the will, it is fatal (Azuela v. CA, Lopez v. Lopez).
• Omission of Statement that Witnesses Signed in the Presence of the Testator and of One Another: FATAL! Cannot be cured by Art. 809 because it cannot be proven by mere visual examination of the will itself without resorting to extrinsic testimonial evidence (Caneda v. CA, Cagro v. Cagro — also fatal if the witnesses failed to sign at the bottom of the attestation clause).

6. Notarial Acknowledgment (Art. 806):

• Every notarial will must be acknowledged before a notary public by the testator and the witnesses.
• A mere jurat ("Subscribed and sworn to before me...") instead of an acknowledgment renders the notarial will VOID (Azuela v. CA).
• The notary public cannot be one of the three (3) instrumental witnesses because he cannot acknowledge before himself (Cruz v. Villasor).
• The testator and witnesses do not need to acknowledge the will simultaneously or in each other's presence before the notary public, provided they all appear before the notary public (Javellana v. Ledesma). However, if the notary public's commission had expired or was outside his territorial jurisdiction, the notarial will is VOID (Guerrero v. Bihis).

#### 3. Special Rules for Deaf, Deaf-Mute, and Blind Testators (Arts. 807–808)

• Deaf or Deaf-Mute Testator (Art. 807): Must personally read the will, if able to do so; otherwise, he shall designate two (2) persons to read it and communicate to him, in some practicable manner, the contents thereof.
• Blind Testator (Art. 808 — Double-Reading Rule): If the testator is blind, the will shall be read to him twice—once, by one of the subscribing witnesses, and again, by the notary public before whom the will is acknowledged.
• Jurisprudential Expansion: Art. 808 also applies to illiterate testators or those with severe visual impairment / cataracts incapable of reading the will (Alvarado v. Gaviola, Garcia v. Vasquez, Guia v. Cosico [2021]).
• Substantial Compliance with Art. 808 (Alvarado v. Gaviola): Where the lawyer/notary read the will aloud once while the testator and all three witnesses listened and followed silently on their own copies, the spirit and purpose of Art. 808 were satisfied.

#### 4. Formalities of a Holographic Will (Arts. 810–814)

1. Requisites (Art. 810): Must be entirely written, dated, and signed by the hand of the testator himself. It is subject to no other form, may be made in or out of the Philippines, and need not be witnessed.

• Partial Date: A date showing only the month and year (e.g., `"FEB./61"`) is valid under the rule of substantial compliance if there is no question of capacity or two competing wills (Roxas v. De Jesus).

2. Probate Witness Requirements (Art. 811):

• Uncontested: At least one (1) witness who knows the handwriting and signature of the testator explicitly declares that the will and signature are in the handwriting of the testator.
• Contested: At least three (3) of such witnesses shall be required. (Note: In Codoy v. Calugay, the SC held the 3-witness requirement mandatory when the authenticity of the handwriting is contested; in Azaola v. Singson and Rivera v. IAC, the SC noted that the court may resort to expert testimony or visual comparison if 3 witnesses are unavailable).
• Photocopy of Lost Holographic Will (Rodelas v. Aranza, Bonilla v. Aranza): A lost or destroyed holographic will cannot be probated by mere testimonial evidence of witnesses (unlike a notarial will), because the handwriting itself is the only guaranty of authenticity; HOWEVER, a photostatic or xerox copy of the lost holographic will may be allowed for probate because the court can still examine the handwriting!

3. Dispositions Below Signature & Dated/Signed Postscripts (Arts. 812–813):

• Dispositions written below the testator's signature must be both dated and signed by the testator to be valid (Art. 812).
• When a number of dispositions appearing in a holographic will are signed without being dated, and the last disposition has a signature and a date, such date validates the dispositions preceding it, whatever be the time of prior dispositions (Art. 813).

4. Insertions, Cancellations, Erasures, or Alterations (Art. 814):

• Must be authenticated by the full signature (not mere initials!) of the testator.
• Effect of Unauthenticated Alteration (Aquino's 3 Scenarios):

1. General Rule: Only the unauthenticated insertion/alteration is void, while the holographic will itself remains valid (Ajero v. CA).

2. Exception 1 (Alteration in Date or Signature): If the unauthenticated alteration affects an essential requisite (the date or the signature), the entire will is void.

3. Exception 2 (Kalaw v. Relova Doctrine): Where the testator originally designated Heir A, crossed out Heir A without full signature authentication, and wrote Heir B, neither inherits: Heir B cannot inherit because the insertion is unauthenticated (Art. 814), and Heir A cannot inherit because the cancellation clearly shows the testator's change of mind (animus revocandi)!

#### 5. Joint Wills Prohibited (Arts. 818–819)

• Joint Will (Art. 818): Two or more persons cannot make a will jointly, or in the same instrument, either for their reciprocal benefit (mutual & joint) or for the benefit of a third person.
• Note on Separate Mutual/Reciprocal Wills: Two separate wills on separate sheets of paper executed by spouses reciprocally instituting each other are VALID (not a joint will).
• Extraterritorial Prohibition on Filipinos (Art. 819): Joint wills executed by Filipinos in a foreign country are VOID in the Philippines, even though authorized by the laws of the country where they may have been executed (public policy exception to lex loci celebrationis).
• If executed by two foreigners abroad where valid: Valid in the Philippines.
• If executed jointly by a Filipino and a Foreigner abroad where valid: Void as to the Filipino, valid as to the Foreigner.

D. Witnesses to Notarial Wills (Arts. 820–824)

• Qualifications (Art. 820):

1. Of sound mind;

2. Aged 18 years or more;

3. Not blind, deaf, or dumb;

4. Able to read and write.

• Disqualifications (Art. 821):

1. Any person not domiciled in the Philippines (Exception under Art. 821/815: Unless the will is executed in a foreign country!);

2. Those who have been convicted of falsification of a document, perjury, or false testimony.

• Supervening Incompetence (Art. 822): If the witnesses are competent at the time of attesting, their becoming subsequently incompetent shall not prevent the allowance of the will.
• Devisee or Legatee as Witness (Art. 823):
• Does NOT disqualify the witness from being a competent witness to the will! The will remains VALID.
• Instead, the devise or legacy given to the witness, or his/her spouse, parent, or child (or anyone claiming under them), is VOID, UNLESS there are three (3) other competent witnesses to such will!
• Wait—what if the witness is instituted as a compulsory heir or given his legitime? His legitime is NOT forfeited (only the free portion devise/legacy is void), because the legitime comes from the law, not the testator's liberality.
• Creditor as Witness (Art. 824): A mere charge on the estate for the payment of debts due at the time of the testator's death does not prevent his creditors from being competent witnesses to his will.

E. Codicils and Incorporation by Reference (Arts. 825–827)

• Codicil (Arts. 825–826): A supplement or addition to a will, made after the execution of a will and annexed to be taken as a part thereof, by which any disposition made in the original will is explained, added to, or altered. It must be executed with all the formalities of a will (either notarial or holographic, regardless of whether the original will was notarial or holographic).
• Incorporation by Reference (Art. 827) — 4 Requisites:

1. The document or paper referred to in the will must be in existence at the time of the execution of the will;

2. The will must clearly describe and identify the same, stating among other things the number of pages thereof;

3. It must be identified by clear and satisfactory proof as the document or paper referred to therein; AND

4. It must be signed by the testator and the witnesses on each and every page, except in case of voluminous books of account or inventories.

• Scope: Only for inventories, lists of properties, or books of account—never to incorporate testamentary dispositions that lack the formalities of a will.

F. Revocation, Republication, Revival, and Probate of Wills (Arts. 828–839)

• Three Modes of Revocation (Art. 830):

1. By Implication of Law (e.g., legal separation where the offending spouse is disqualified by operation of law under Art. 63(4) FC; declaration of nullity/annulment of marriage with bad faith spouse under Arts. 43/50 FC; preterition under Art. 854; transformation, alienation, or loss of thing bequeathed under Art. 957; unworthiness under Art. 1032).

2. By Some Will, Codicil, or Other Writing Executed as Provided in Case of Wills (Express revocation or Implied revocation due to irreconcilable inconsistency).

3. By Overt Physical Act (Burning, Tearing, Canceling, or Obliterating):

• Requisites: (a) Testamentary capacity at time of revocation; (b) Animus revocandi (intent to revoke); (c) Physical act of burning, tearing, canceling, or obliterating (even slight scorching of the paper suffices if with animus revocandi, but if thrown in the stove and rescued before the paper itself is burned, no revocation, Lipana); (d) Done by the testator himself, or by another person in his presence and by his express direction.
• Unauthorized Destruction (Art. 830, last par.): If burned, torn, canceled, or obliterated by another person without the express direction of the testator, the will may still be established (probated), and the estate distributed in accordance therewith, if its contents, due execution, and the fact of unauthorized destruction are established according to the Rules of Court.
• Presumption of Revocation: Where a will traced to the possession of the testator cannot be found after his death, it is presumed that the testator destroyed it with animus revocandi (Gago v. Mamuyac).
• Doctrine of Dependent Relative Revocation (Molo v. Molo):
• Where the act of destruction/revocation of Will #1 is connected with the making of Will #2 so as fairly to raise the inference that the testator meant the revocation of Will #1 to depend upon the efficacy of Will #2, and Will #2 fails to take effect (e.g., due to formal defects), the revocation of Will #1 is inoperative and Will #1 remains in force!
• Compare with Art. 832: A revocation made in a subsequent will shall take effect, even if the new will should become inoperative by reason of the incapacity of the heirs, devisees or legatees, or by their renunciation (because the new will itself is formally valid!).
• Revocation Based on a False Cause (Art. 833): A revocation of a will based on a false cause or an illegal cause is null and void (provided the false cause appears on the face of the revoking instrument).
• Effect of Revocation on Recognition of Illegitimate Child (Art. 834): The recognition of an illegitimate child does not lose its legal effect, even though the will wherein it was made should be revoked (because recognition is an irrevocable admission of filiation, not a testamentary disposition!).
• Republication and Revival (Arts. 835–837):
• Art. 835 (Will Void as to Form): The testator cannot republish, without reproducing in a subsequent will, the dispositions contained in a previous one which is void as to its form (a mere codicil referring to a formally void will cannot validate it!).
• Art. 836 (Will Void for Non-Formal Defect, e.g., Underage/Insane/Vitiated Consent): Execution of a codicil referring to a previous will has the effect of republishing the will as modified by the codicil.
• Art. 837 (Revival of Will 1 after Revocation of Will 2):
• If Will 2 EXPRESSLY revoked Will 1, and the testator later revokes Will 2, Will 1 is NOT revived (unless republished by another will/codicil).
• If Will 2 IMPLIEDLY revoked Will 1 (merely inconsistent dispositions), and the testator later revokes Will 2, Will 1 IS automatically revived!
• Probate of Wills (Arts. 838–839):
• Art. 838: No will shall pass either real or personal property unless it is proved and allowed in accordance with the Rules of Court.
• Statute of Limitations Does NOT Apply: Probate is mandatory and imprescriptible (Guevara v. Guevara, Gallanosa v. Arcangel).
• Scope of Inquiry in Probate: Limited to extrinsic validity (1. Due execution in accordance with formalities; 2. Testamentary capacity; 3. Voluntariness/absence of vices of consent).
• Exception (Nepomuceno v. CA, Nuguid v. Nuguid, Balanay v. Martinez): For practical considerations, the probate court may pass upon the intrinsic validity of the will at the outset if the defect is apparent on the face of the will and probate would become an idle ceremony (e.g., universal preterition of compulsory heirs in Nuguid, or a void disposition to a concubine on the face of the will in Nepomuceno).

III. INSTITUTION OF HEIRS, PRETERITION, SUBSTITUTION, & CONDITIONAL DISPOSITIONS (Arts. 840–885)

A. Institution of Heirs (Arts. 840–856)

• Three Principles of Institution:

1. Equality (Art. 846): Heirs instituted without designation of shares shall inherit in equal parts.

• Crucial Qualification when Compulsory Heirs Concur with a Stranger (Aquino's Rule & 2026 Bar Q#20): When the testator institutes his compulsory heirs together with a stranger without designating shares (e.g., "I institute my two legitimate children A and B, and my friend C, as my universal heirs"), the equality rule under Art. 846 applies ONLY to the Free Portion! Why? Because the legitime is reserved by law for compulsory heirs (Art. 886). Therefore:

1. First, separate and give the legitime to the compulsory heirs (A and B get 1/2 of the estate, or 1/4 each);

2. Second, divide the Free Portion (1/2) equally among A, B, and C (each gets 1/3 of the 1/2 Free Portion = 1/6 of the estate)!

2. Individuality (Art. 847): When the testator institutes some heirs individually and others collectively (e.g., "I institute A, B, and the children of C"), those collectively designated shall be considered as individually instituted, unless it clearly appears that the intention of the testator was otherwise.

3. Simultaneity (Art. 849): When the testator calls to the succession a person and his children (e.g., "I institute A and his children"), they are all deemed to have been instituted simultaneously and not successively.

• False Cause in Institution (Art. 850): The statement of a false cause for the institution of an heir shall be considered as not written (the institution remains VALID), UNLESS it appears from the will that the testator would not have made such institution if he had known the falsity of such cause (Austria v. Reyes).

B. Preterition (Art. 854) vs. Ineffective Disinheritance (Art. 918)

• Definition of Preterition (Art. 854): The omission in the testator's will of one, some, or all of the compulsory heirs in the direct line, whether living at the time of the execution of the will or born after the death of the testator (posthumous child).
• Three Strict Requisites of Preterition:

1. Total Omission from the Inheritance: The heir receives nothing under the will (as heir, devisee, or legatee), received nothing by way of donation inter vivos (advance on legitime), AND there is no undisposed portion of the estate left for intestacy! If the heir received even a small legacy, or a donation inter vivos, or if the will disposed of only part of the estate leaving an intestate portion, there is NO preterition—his remedy is merely completion of legitime (actio ad supplendam legitimam) under Art. 906 (Reyes v. Barretto-Datu, Seangio v. Reyes, Aznar v. Duncan).

2. Omitted Heir is a Compulsory Heir in the Direct Line:

• Included: Legitimate children and descendants, illegitimate (nonmarital) children and descendants, legitimate parents and ascendants (when there are no descendants), AND adopted children (Acain v. IAC, R.A. 11642).
• EXCLUDED: The Surviving Spouse! Omission of the surviving spouse does NOT constitute preterition because the spouse is not in the direct line (Acain v. IAC, Nuguid v. Nuguid). If the surviving spouse is omitted, the institution of heirs is NOT annulled; it is merely reduced to give the surviving spouse his/her legitime.

3. Omitted Heir Survives the Testator (Art. 854, par. 2): If the omitted compulsory heir dies before the testator, the institution shall be effectual, without prejudice to the right of representation (if the omitted child left descendants, the preterition still operates through his representing descendants!).

Feature Preterition (Art. 854) Ineffective Disinheritance (Art. 918) Incomplete Legitime (Art. 906)
Nature of Act Total omission (intentional or unintentional) from the inheritance. Testator expressly disinherits the compulsory heir, but without specifying the cause, or for a cause not in the law, or whose truth is not proved/contradicted. Compulsory heir is given less than his full legitime (by will, donation, or partial intestacy).
Who is Covered Compulsory heirs in the direct line ONLY (excludes surviving spouse). ANY compulsory heir (includes the surviving spouse!). ANY compulsory heir.
Effect on Institution of Heirs Annuls the institution of heirs IN TOTO (total intestacy as to the instituted shares). Annuls the institution of heirs ONLY insofar as it prejudices the person disinherited (free portion dispositions remain valid!). Institution of heirs remains valid, reduced only if needed to complete the legitime.
Effect on Devises and Legacies Remain VALID insofar as they are not inofficious (do not impair the legitime). Remain VALID insofar as they do not impair the legitime. Reduced only if the institution reduction is insufficient to cover the legitime (Art. 907).

C. Substitution of Heirs (Arts. 857–870)

1. Simple or Vulgar Substitution (Art. 859): The testator designates one or more persons to substitute the heir or heirs instituted in case such heir or heirs should:

• (a) Die before him (Predecease);
• (b) Not wish to accept (Renunciation/Repudiation); OR
• (c) Be incapacitated to accept (Incapacity).
• Rule: A simple substitution, without a statement of the cases to which it refers, shall comprise all three (3) mentioned above, unless the testator has otherwise provided.

2. Brief or Compendious Substitution (Art. 860): Two or more persons substituted for one (brief), or one person for two or more heirs (compendious).

3. Reciprocal Substitution (Art. 861): Instituted heirs are also made substitutes of each other; if instituted in unequal shares, they shall have the same relative proportions in the substitution as in the institution, unless the intention of the testator clearly appears otherwise.

4. Fideicommissary Substitution (Arts. 863–869):

• Definition (Art. 863): By virtue of which the fiduciary (first heir) instituted is entrusted with the obligation to preserve and to transmit to a second heir (fideicommissary) the whole or part of the inheritance.
• Strict Requisites for Validity:

1. There must be a first heir (fiduciary) and a second heir (fideicommissary);

2. An express obligation to preserve and transmit the property (if there is no obligation to preserve—e.g., the first heir is allowed to alienate or consume the property and only transmit whatever remains at his death, fideicommissum de residuo—it is NOT a fideicommissary substitution, Crisologo v. Singson, PCIB v. Escolin);

3. The substitution must not go beyond one (1) degree from the heir originally instituted (meaning parent or child of the fiduciary—never a brother, sister, or stranger, Ramirez v. Ramirez, Palacios v. Ramirez);

4. Both the fiduciary and the fideicommissary must be living (or at least conceived) at the time of the death of the testator;

5. Must be expressly made (either by naming it fideicommissary or imposing the explicit obligation to preserve and transmit, Art. 867[1]); AND

6. Can never burden the legitime (Art. 864, 872)—it can only be imposed on the free portion!

• When Fideicommissary Acquires Right (Art. 866): The second heir acquires a right to the succession from the time of the testator's death, even though he should die before the fiduciary. The right of the second heir passes to his own heirs!
• Effect if Fideicommissary Clause is Void (Art. 868): The nullity of the fideicommissary substitution does not prejudice the validity of the institution of the heirs first designated; the fideicommissary clause shall simply be considered as not written!

D. Conditional Dispositions and Dispositions with a Term (Arts. 871–885)

• No Condition on the Legitime (Art. 872): The testator cannot impose any charge, condition, or substitution whatsoever upon the legitimes prescribed in this Code. Should he do so, the same shall be considered as not imposed. (Sole Exception: Art. 1083 allows the testator to prohibit the partition of the estate, including the legitime, for a period not exceeding 20 years).
• Impossible or Illegal Conditions (Art. 873): Impossible conditions and those contrary to law or good customs shall be considered as not imposed and shall in no manner prejudice the heir, devisee, or legatee, even if the testator should otherwise provide. (Contrast with Obligations & Contracts under Art. 1183, where an impossible condition annuls the obligation itself!)
• Condition Not to Marry (Art. 874):
• Absolute condition not to contract a first or subsequent marriage: Shall be considered as not written (VOID), UNLESS such condition has been imposed on the widow or widower by the deceased spouse, or by the latter's ascendants or descendants!
• Relative prohibition to marry (e.g., not to marry a specific person, or before a certain reasonable age) is generally VALID.
• Right of Usufruct or Allowance During Unmarried State (Art. 874, par. 2): A right of usufruct, or an allowance or some personal prestation may validly be devised or bequeathed to any person for the time during which he or she should remain unmarried or in widowhood.
• Disposicion Captatoria (Art. 875): Any disposition made upon the condition that the heir shall make some provision in his will in favor of the testator or of any other person shall be VOID (both the condition and the disposition itself are void!).
• Suspensive Condition vs. Suspensive Term / Day Certain (Arts. 878, 885, 1034 par. 3 — Tested in 2026 Bar Q#18!):
Feature Suspensive Condition (Arts. 871, 885, 1034 par. 3) Suspensive Term / Day Certain (Arts. 878, 885)
Nature of Event Future and uncertain event (may or may not happen). Future and certain event (must necessarily come, although it may not be known when, e.g., death of a person, Art. 1193).
When Right Vests Vests only upon fulfillment of the condition. Vests immediately upon the testator's death, though demandability/enjoyment is suspended until the term arrives.
Effect if Instituted Heir Dies AFTER Testator but BEFORE Condition/Term Arrives Acquires NOTHING and transmits NOTHING to his own heirs (must be living at BOTH the testator's death AND the fulfillment of the condition, Art. 1034 par. 3). Acquires his right and TRANSMITS it to his own heirs even before the arrival of the term (Art. 878)! (Exact doctrine of 2026 Bar Q#18!)
Who Holds the Property in the Meantime (Art. 885 / 880) Placed under administration (Art. 880) until the condition is fulfilled or becomes certain it cannot be fulfilled. Delivered to the intestate (legal) heirs (unless the testator granted a usufruct to another, as in 2026 Bar Q#18), who must give security (caucion) to the instituted heir (Art. 885, par. 2).
• Caucion Muciana (Arts. 879–880, 882):
• Applies in two cases:

1. Potestative Negative Condition (Art. 879): Condition is that the heir/devisee/legatee shall not do or not give something—he receives the property immediately upon giving a security (caucion Muciana) that he will return the property with its fruits and interests if he contravenes the condition.

2. Modal Institution (Institucion Sub Modo — Art. 882): Statement of the object of the institution, or the application of the property left by the testator, or the charge imposed by him, shall not be considered a condition unless it appears that such was his intention. That which has been left in this manner may be claimed at once, provided the instituted heir gives security for compliance with the wishes of the testator and for the return of anything he may receive, together with its fruits and interests, if he should disregard this obligation.

IV. LEGITIMES, COMPULSORY HEIRS, RESERVA TRONCAL, & DISINHERITANCE (Arts. 886–923)

A. Concept of Legitime and Classes of Compulsory Heirs (Arts. 886–887)

• Legitime (Art. 886): That part of the testator's property which he cannot dispose of because the law has reserved it for certain heirs who are, therefore, called compulsory heirs.
• Classes of Compulsory Heirs (Art. 887):

1. Primary Compulsory Heirs: Legitimate children and descendants, with respect to their legitimate parents and ascendants (includes legally adopted children under R.A. 11642 / Art. 189 FC).

2. Secondary Compulsory Heirs:

• (a) Legitimate parents and ascendants, with respect to their legitimate children and descendants (inherit only in default of legitimate children and descendants).
• (b) Illegitimate parents, with respect to their illegitimate decedent child under Art. 903 (inherit only in default of ALL descendants, whether legitimate or illegitimate!).

3. Concurring Compulsory Heirs: (Succeed together with primary or secondary heirs, or alone in default of them)

• (a) The Widow or Widower (Surviving Spouse — must be legally married, or a spouse in good faith in a marriage terminated by death before annulment; in legal separation, the innocent spouse retains legitime while the guilty spouse is disqualified);
• (b) Illegitimate (Nonmarital) Children and their descendants (note: Under Art. 895 as amended by Art. 176 of the Family Code, the legitime of each illegitimate child consists of one-half (1/2) of the legitime of a legitimate child).

B. MASTER TABLE OF LEGITIMES (TESTATE SUCCESSION — Arts. 888–903)

> Golden Rules for Computing Legitimes (Aquino's "Math of Law"):

> 1. Rule of Exclusion: Legitimate children/descendants exclude legitimate parents/ascendants. Any child/descendant (legitimate or illegitimate) excludes illegitimate parents (Art. 903).

> 2. 1/2 Collective Legitime of Legitimate Children (Art. 888): Whether there is 1 legitimate child or 10 legitimate children, their collective legitime is ALWAYS 1/2 of the net hereditary estate, divided equally among them!

> 3. Surviving Spouse's Share with Legitimate Children:

> * With 1 legitimate child $\rightarrow$ Surviving Spouse gets 1/4 (taken from the Free Portion, Art. 892 par. 1).

> * With 2 or more legitimate children $\rightarrow$ Surviving Spouse gets a share equal to the legitime of 1 legitimate child (taken from the Free Portion, Art. 892 par. 2).

> 4. Illegitimate Child's Share with Legitimate Children (Art. 176 FC / Art. 895 NCC):

> * Each illegitimate child gets 1/2 of the legitime of 1 legitimate child (taken from the Free Portion).

C. Reserva Troncal (Art. 891)

• Codal Rule (Art. 891): The ascendant who inherits from his descendant any property which the latter may have acquired by gratuitous title from another ascendant, or a brother or sister, is obliged to reserve such property as he may have acquired by operation of law for the benefit of relatives who are within the third degree and who belong to the line from which said property came.
• Purpose: To prevent property from passing out of a family line (paternal or maternal) by the accident of two transmissions without issue.
• The Four (4) Parties in Reserva Troncal:

1. Origin (Mediate Source): An ascendant, or a brother or sister (must be a legitimate relative) who transfers the property to the Prepositus by gratuitous (lucrative) title (donation or succession—no consideration).

2. Prepositus (Propositus): The descendant (or brother/sister) who acquires the property from the Origin by gratuitous title and then dies without legitimate issue (he is the arbiter of the reserva—if he sells or devises the property before dying, no reserva arises!).

3. Reservista (Obligor): Another ascendant of the Prepositus who acquires the property from the Prepositus by operation of law (either as legitime or by intestate succession—NOT by testamentary free portion or donation!).

4. Reservatarios (Beneficiaries): Relatives of the Prepositus who are:

• (a) Within the third (3rd) degree of consanguinity reckoned from the Prepositus (Mendoza v. Policarpio);
• (b) Belonging to the line of the Origin; AND
• (c) Related to both the Origin and the Prepositus legitimately.
• Who are relatives within the 3rd degree of the Prepositus?
• 1st Degree: Father or Mother (of the Origin's line)
• 2nd Degree: Grandparents, Brothers and Sisters (full or half-blood of the Origin's line; full-blood gets double the share of half-blood, Padura v. Baldovino)
• 3rd Degree: Great-grandparents, Uncles and Aunts, Nephews and Nieces (children of brothers/sisters of the Prepositus). First cousins are 4th degree and are EXCLUDED (Mendoza v. Policarpio)!
• Rule of Preference among Reservatarios: Controlled by the rules of intestate succession (direct line excludes collateral line; nearer degree excludes farther degree, subject to representation by nephews/nieces within the 3rd degree, Florentino v. Florentino).

D. Computation of the Net Hereditary Estate & Reduction of Inofficious Dispositions (Arts. 908–911)

• Three-Step Formula under Art. 908 (Aquino's Step-by-Step Method):

1. Step 1: Determine the value of the property left at the death of the testator (Gross Estate);

2. Step 2: Deduct all debts and charges (not including charges imposed in the will itself) $\rightarrow$ gives the Net Estate at Death;

3. Step 3: Add the value of all collatable donations inter vivos (assessed at their value at the time the donation was made, Art. 1071) $\rightarrow$ gives the Theoretical Net Hereditary Estate (NHE) upon which the Legitimes are computed!

• Imputation of Donations Inter Vivos (Arts. 909–910):
• Donation to a Compulsory Heir (Child/Descendant): Imputed (charged) first to his Legitime (treated as an advance on legitime, unless the donor expressly provided that it is not collatable/charged to the free portion); if it exceeds his legitime, the excess is charged to the Free Portion; if it still exceeds the Free Portion, it is reduced as inofficious!
• Donation to a Stranger: Charged directly to the Free Portion; if it exceeds the Free Portion, it is reduced as inofficious!
• Order of Reduction to Preserve the Legitime (Art. 911):

1. First, reduce/suppress testamentary dispositions (legacies and devises):

• Non-preferred legacies/devises are reduced pro rata first;
• Preferred legacies/devises (so declared by the testator) are reduced next. (Note: When reducing to protect the legitime or when donations inter vivos concur with legacies/devises, apply Art. 911; when there are NO donations and NO legitime impairment—only the estate/free portion is insufficient to cover all legacies/devises—apply the 6-tier order in Art. 950!)

2. Second, if suppressing all legacies/devises is still not enough to cover the legitime, reduce donations inter vivos in inverse chronological order (newest / most recent donations are reduced or revoked first, respecting older donations pursuant to the irrepealability of donations!).

E. Disinheritance (Arts. 915–923)

• Six Essential Requisites of a Valid Disinheritance (Arts. 915–917):

1. Made in a valid will (Art. 916);

2. For a cause expressly stated by law (Arts. 919, 920, 921—exclusive enumeration);

3. The cause must be expressly specified in the will itself (Art. 916);

4. The cause must be existing at the time of the execution of the will;

5. The cause must be true and certain (not conditional or preventive; if contradicted by the disinherited heir, the other heirs have the burden of proving its truth, Art. 917); AND

6. Must be total and unconditional (cannot be partial disinheritance).

• Statutory Grounds for Disinheritance:
• Of Children/Descendants (Art. 919 — 8 Grounds): (1) Attempt against life of testator, spouse, descendants, or ascendants (requires final conviction); (2) Groundless accusation of a crime punishable by imprisonment for 6 years or more; (3) Conviction of adultery or concubinage with the spouse of the testator; (4) Fraud, violence, intimidation, or undue influence causing the testator to make or change a will; (5) Refusal without justifiable cause to support the parent/ascendant; (6) Maltreatment of the testator by word or deed by the child/descendant (no criminal conviction required, Seangio v. Reyes); (7) Leading a dishonorable or disgraceful life; (8) Conviction of a crime which carries the penalty of civil interdiction.
• Of Parents/Ascendants (Art. 920 — 8 Grounds): Includes numbers (1), (2), (3), (4), (5) above, plus: (6) Abandonment of children or inducing daughters to lead a corrupt/immoral life, or attempting against their virtue; (7) Loss of parental authority for causes specified in the Code; (8) Attempt by one of the parents against the life of the other, unless there has been a reconciliation between them.
• Of the Spouse (Art. 921 — 6 Grounds): Includes numbers (1), (2), (4), (5) above, plus: (3) When the spouse has given cause for legal separation (no judicial decree of legal separation is required!); (4) When the spouse has given grounds for the loss of parental authority.
• Reconciliation (Art. 922): A subsequent reconciliation between the offender and the offended person deprives the latter of the right to disinherit, and renders ineffectual any disinheritance that may have been made. (Must be mutual forgiveness/restoration of relationship, not a mere general Christian pardon on the deathbed).
• Right of Representation in Valid Disinheritance (Art. 923): The children and descendants of the person validly disinherited shall take his or her place and shall preserve the rights of compulsory heirs with respect to the legitime; but the disinherited parent shall not have the usufruct or administration of the property which constitutes the legitime. (Note: They represent the disinherited heir ONLY as to the legitime, unless the entire estate goes by intestacy).

V. LEGACIES AND DEVISES (Arts. 924–959)

• Order of Payment When Estate / Free Portion is Insufficient (Art. 950 vs. Art. 911):
• When Art. 950 Applies (No compulsory heirs, or legitimes are already satisfied and there are no collatable donations, and the remaining estate cannot pay all legacies/devises):

1. Remuneratory legacies or devises;

2. Legacies or devises declared by the testator to be preferential;

3. Legacies for support;

4. Legacies for education;

5. Legacies or devises of a specific, determinate thing which forms a part of the estate;

6. All others pro rata.

• Revocation of Legacies and Devises by Operation of Law (Art. 957):

1. Transformation: If the testator transforms the thing bequeathed in such a manner that it does not retain either the form or the denomination it had;

2. Alienation: If the testator by any title or for any cause alienates the thing bequeathed or any part thereof (unless it reverts to the testator through nullity of the contract or repurchase under a pacto de retro sale, Art. 957 par. 2);

3. Total Loss: If the thing bequeathed is totally lost during the lifetime of the testator, or after his death without the heir's fault.

VI. LEGAL OR INTESTATE SUCCESSION (Arts. 960–1014)

A. Fundamental Rules & Right of Representation (Arts. 960–977)

• Order of Intestate Succession (Art. 978–1014):

1. Legitimate Children and Descendants (with concurring Illegitimate Children and Surviving Spouse);

2. Legitimate Parents and Ascendants (with concurring Illegitimate Children and Surviving Spouse);

3. Illegitimate Children and Descendants AND Surviving Spouse (concurring with each other, or alone; Note: Surviving Spouse also concurs with Brothers, Sisters, Nephews, and Nieces under Art. 1001!);

4. Collateral Relatives:

• First, Brothers, Sisters, Nephews, and Nieces (2nd and 3rd degrees — preferred collateral relatives);
• Second, Other Collateral Relatives up to the Fifth (5th) Degree of consanguinity (Art. 1010 — e.g., uncles/aunts [3rd], first cousins [4th], children of first cousins / great-uncles [5th]);

5. The State (Escheat — Arts. 1011–1014).

• Right of Representation (Arts. 970–977):
• Definition (Art. 970): A right created by fiction of law, by virtue of which the representative is raised to the place and the degree of the person represented, and acquires the rights which the latter would have if he were living or if he could have inherited.
• When Representation Takes Place:
• In Intestacy: (1) Predecease, (2) Incapacity / Unworthiness, (3) Disinheritance.
• In Testacy (Compulsory Succession): Same three grounds, ONLY with respect to the Legitime! There is NO right of representation for a voluntary heir or with respect to the Free Portion!
• NEVER in Repudiation/Renunciation (Art. 977): An heir who repudiates his inheritance cannot be represented! However, a person who renounced his own inheritance from X may still represent X in the inheritance of Y (Art. 976), because in representation, the representative succeeds directly the decedent (Y), not the person represented (X) (Art. 971)!
• In What Lines Representation Takes Place (Arts. 972, 975):

1. Direct Descending Line: Always takes place (ad infinitum).

2. Direct Ascending Line: NEVER takes place (nearer ascendant always excludes farther ascendant, Art. 972).

3. Collateral Line: Takes place ONLY in favor of the children of brothers or sisters (nephews and nieces), whether of the full or half blood! Grandnephews/grandnieces and cousins have NO right of representation (Bagunu v. Piedad).

• Art. 975 Rule for Nephews and Nieces:
• If nephews and nieces concur with at least one surviving uncle or aunt (brother/sister of the decedent) $\rightarrow$ they inherit by representation (per stirpes).
• If ONLY nephews and nieces survive (all brothers and sisters predeceased or are incapacitated) $\rightarrow$ they inherit in their own right (per capita / equal shares) (subject to the 2:1 full-blood vs. half-blood ratio)! (Note: If all brothers and sisters repudiated, the nephews and nieces inherit in their own right as next in degree under Art. 969, NOT by representation!)
• The Iron Curtain Rule (Art. 992) & The Landmark Aquino v. Aquino Doctrine (G.R. No. 208912 & G.R. No. 209018, Dec. 7, 2021, En Banc):
• Traditional Rule (Art. 992): "An illegitimate child has no right to inherit ab intestato from the legitimate children and relatives of his father or mother; nor shall such children or relatives inherit in the same manner from the illegitimate child." (Formerly interpreted in Diaz v. IAC to bar an illegitimate child from representing his legitimate parent in the estate of his grandparent, and vice-versa).
• LANDMARK ABANDONMENT IN AQUINO v. AQUINO (Dec. 7, 2021, En Banc — Examinable in the 2026 Bar!):
• The Supreme Court En Banc abandoned Diaz v. IAC and reinterpreted Articles 982 and 992 of the Civil Code!
• New Controlling Doctrine: A child whose parents did not marry each other (nonmarital/illegitimate grandchild) CAN inherit from their grandparent by right of representation of their predeceased parent (whether marital or nonmarital), and similarly, a marital grandchild can represent a nonmarital parent in the grandparent's estate!
• Scope of Aquino v. Aquino: The Court expressly limited this ruling to grandchildren and descendants in the direct descending line exercising the right of representation under Art. 982; as to collateral relatives (e.g., illegitimate brother vs. legitimate brother), Art. 992 was not the subject of the representation reinterpretation.

VII. PROVISIONS COMMON TO TESTATE AND INTESTATE SUCCESSION (Arts. 1015–1105)

A. Right of Accretion (Arts. 1015–1023)

• Definition (Art. 1015): A right by virtue of which, when two or more persons are called to the same inheritance, devise or legacy, the part assigned to the one who renounces or cannot receive his share, or who died before the testator, is added or incorporated to that of his co-heirs, co-devisees, or co-legatees.
• Requisites of Accretion in Testamentary Succession (Art. 1016):

1. Plurality of subjects: Two or more persons are called to the same inheritance, or to the same portion thereof, pro indiviso (not earmarked/segregated into specific determinate properties; note that words like "in equal shares" or "one-half each" do NOT exclude accretion under Art. 1017!); AND

2. Vacancy of share: One of the persons thus called dies before the testator (predecease), renounces the inheritance (repudiation), or cannot receive it (incapacity), and there is no substitute or right of representation that takes precedence.

• THE GOLDEN RULE OF ART. 1021 (Legitime vs. Free Portion — Tested in 2026 Bar Q#20!):
• "Among the compulsory heirs the right of accretion takes place only when the free portion is left to two or more of them, or to any one of them and to a stranger."
• "Should the part repudiated be the legitime, the other co-heirs shall succeed to it in their own right, and not by the right of accretion."
• How this works in practice (2026 Bar Q#20):
• Testator George has a net estate of PHP 360M and institutes his two legitimate daughters (Ann and Juliene) and his friend (Gary) as universal heirs.
• Initial Shares:
• Collective Legitime of Ann & Juliene = $1/2 \times 360\text{M} = 180\text{M}$ (90M each).
• Free Portion = $180\text{M}$, divided equally among Ann, Juliene, and Gary (Art. 846) = 60M each.
• Juliene Repudiates:
• There is NO representation in repudiation (Art. 977).
• Juliene's 90M Legitime goes exclusively to Ann in her own right as co-compulsory heir (Art. 1021, par. 2 — Gary gets zero from the legitime!).
• Juliene's 60M Free Portion share goes by accretion equally to Ann and Gary (Art. 1021, par. 1 — 30M to Ann, 30M to Gary).
• Final Distribution: Ann = 270M ($90\text{M} + 90\text{M} + 60\text{M} + 30\text{M}$); Gary = 90M ($60\text{M} + 30\text{M}$); Juliene = 0!

B. Capacity to Succeed & Disqualifications (Arts. 1024–1040)

• Absolute Incapacity (Arts. 1025–1026):

1. Persons not living or at least conceived at the moment the succession opens (Art. 1025, in rel. to Art. 40–41 on intra-uterine life);

2. Abortive infants;

3. Associations or corporations not permitted by law to inherit.

• Relative Incapacity Based on Undue Influence (Art. 1027 — Applies ONLY to Testamentary Free Portion, NOT Intestacy or Legitime!):

1. Priest or minister of the gospel who heard the confession of the testator during his last illness, or the minister who extended spiritual aid to him during the same period (plus chapter, church, order, or relatives within the 4th degree of such priest);

2. Guardian with respect to testamentary dispositions given by a ward before the final accounts of the guardianship have been approved (Exception: Unless the guardian is an ascendant, descendant, brother, sister, or spouse of the ward!);

3. Attesting witness to the execution of a will, the spouse, parents, or children, or any one claiming under such witness, spouse, parents, or children (unless there are 3 other competent witnesses, Art. 823);

4. Physician, surgeon, nurse, health officer, or druggist who took care of the testator during the last illness;

5. Individuals, associations, and corporations not permitted by law to inherit.

• Relative Incapacity Based on Morality / Public Policy (Art. 1028 in rel. to Art. 739):

1.Those made between persons who were guilty of adultery or concubinage at the time of the execution of the will (no criminal conviction required; preponderance of evidence suffices, Nepomuceno v. CA);

2. Those made between persons found guilty of the same criminal offense, in consideration thereof;

3. Those made to a public officer or his wife, descendants, and ascendants, by reason of his office.

• Incapacity by Reason of Unworthiness (Art. 1032 — Applies to BOTH Testate and Intestate Succession!):

1. Parents who have abandoned their children or induced their daughters to lead a corrupt or immoral life, or attempted against their virtue;

2. Any person who has been convicted of an attempt against the life of the testator, his or her spouse, descendants, or ascendants;

3. Any person who has accused the testator of a crime for which the law prescribes imprisonment for 6 years or more, if the accusation has been found groundless;

4. Any heir of full age who, having knowledge of the violent death of the testator, should fail to report it to an officer of the law within a month, unless the authorities have already taken action (Note:Since Philippine law imposes no legal obligation on an heir to report a crime under the RPC, this ground is practically inoperative under the second sentence of Art. 1032(4): "This prohibition shall not apply to cases wherein, according to law, there is no obligation to make an accusation");

5. Any person convicted of adultery or concubinage with the spouse of the testator;

6. Any person who by fraud, violence, intimidation, or undue influence should cause the testator to make a will or to change one already made;

7. Any person who by the same means prevents another from making a will, or from revoking one already made, or who supplants, conceals, or alters the latter's will;

8. Any person who falsifies or forges a supposed will of the decedent.

• Condonation / Pardon of Unworthiness (Art. 1033):
• Implied Condonation: If the testator knew of the cause of unworthiness at the time of making the will and still instituted the unworthy person.
• Express Condonation: If the testator learned of it subsequently (or in intestate succession), he must condone it in writing in a public document!
• Prescriptive Period to Declare Incapacity and Recover Inheritance (Art. 1040): Within five (5) years from the time the disqualified person took possession of the inheritance, devise, or legacy.

C. Acceptance, Repudiation, Collation, and Partition (Arts. 1041–1105)

• Forms of Acceptance vs. Repudiation (Arts. 1049–1052):
• Acceptance: May be express (public or private document) or tacit (acts of ownership/assignment, Art. 1050) or presumed (failure to signify acceptance or repudiation within 30 days after court order of distribution, Art. 1057).
• Repudiation (Art. 1051): Must ALWAYS be express—made either in a public or authentic instrument, or by petition presented to the court having jurisdiction over the testamentary or intestate proceedings! Oral or private-instrument repudiation is VOID.
• Creditors' Remedy When Heir Repudiates to Their Prejudice (Art. 1052): The creditors may petition the court to authorize them to accept the inheritance in the name of the heir. However, the acceptance shall benefit the creditors only to the extent sufficient to cover the amount of their credits. The excess shall NOT pertain to the renouncer, but shall be adjudicated to the persons to whom, in accordance with the rules of this Code, it may belong!
• Partition and Distribution (Arts. 1078–1105):
• Prohibition to Partition: Generally, no co-heir may be compelled to remain in the indivision of the estate, except when the testator expressly forbade partition for a period not exceeding 20 years (Art. 1083), or as to the Family Home (which continues for 10 years after the death of the person constituting it or as long as there is a minor beneficiary, Art. 159 FC).
• Legal Redemption by Co-Heirs (Art. 1088): Should any of the heirs sell his hereditary rights to a stranger before the partition, any or all of the co-heirs may be subrogated to the rights of the purchaser by reimbursing him for the price of the sale, provided they do so within the period of one (1) month from the time they were notified in writing by the vendor of the sale!
• Preterition of a Compulsory Heir in the Partition (Art. 1104): A partition made with preterition of any of the compulsory heirs shall not be rescinded, unless it be proved that there was bad faith or fraud on the part of the other persons interested; but the latter shall be proportionately obliged to pay to the person omitted the share which belongs to him.
• Inclusion of a Stranger in the Partition (Art. 1105): A partition which includes a person believed to be an heir, but who is not, shall be VOID only with respect to such person!
