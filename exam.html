CIVIL LAW REVIEW Q#1 [Easy — Persons & Conflict of Laws: Effectivity of Laws & Publication (Art. 2 NCC)]
Congress enacted Republic Act No. 12999, which states in its final section: 'This Act shall take effect immediately upon its approval by the President, without need of publication.' The President signed the law on June 1, 2025, and an administrative agency enforced it on June 2, 2025 before any publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation. Under Art. 2 of the Civil Code and Judge Timoteo Aquino's Reviewer, is the enforcement on June 2, 2025 valid?
A. Yes, because the legislature has plenary power to dispense with publication through the phrase 'unless it is otherwise provided' in Article 2 of the Civil Code.
B. No, because publication is an indispensable requirement of constitutional due process; the clause 'unless it is otherwise provided' refers solely to the 15-day period and never to the requirement of publication itself.
C. Yes, provided the law is not penal in nature and is posted on the official website of the Official Gazette.
D. No, because a law can never take effect in less than 15 days from publication even if Congress provides a shorter period.
⚡ 5-SEC RULE: Publication is ALWAYS mandatory! 'Unless otherwise provided' only changes the 15-day waiting period, never the publication requirement (Tañada v. Tuvera).
✅ Correct Answer: B (Art. 2, Civil Code; Tañada v. Tuvera (G.R. No. L-63915); Aquino, Reviewer on Civil Law (3rd Ed.))
1. Answer: No, the enforcement of R.A. No. 12999 on June 2, 2025 without prior publication is void.
2. Legal Basis: Under Article 2 of the Civil Code, laws shall take effect after fifteen (15) days following the completion of their publication either in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation in the Philippines, unless it is otherwise provided. In Tañada v. Tuvera, the Supreme Court held that 'unless it is otherwise provided' refers solely to the 15-day period and never to the requirement of publication.
3. Application: Here, while Congress could validly shorten the 15-day period so that the law takes effect immediately upon publication, it could not dispense with publication altogether without violating due process.
4. Conclusion: Therefore, R.A. No. 12999 had not yet taken effect on June 2, 2025.

✅ Correct Answer: B (Art. 16(2) & Art. 1039, Civil Code; Bellis v. Bellis (G.R. No. L-23678); Aquino, Reviewer on Civil Law (3rd Ed.))

4. Conclusion: Thus, Charlie cannot invoke Philippine legitime rules, and Bob takes the entire estate.

Upon Dante's death, Lorna claims a one-half (1/2) co-ownership share over the Quezon City house and lot on account of her household care. Is Lorna's claim valid?
A. Yes, under Article 147 of the Family Code, a partner whose efforts consisted in the care and maintenance of the family and household is deemed to have contributed jointly.
B. No, because Dante and Lorna were incapacitated to marry each other; thus, Article 148 of the Family Code governs, under which only properties acquired through their ACTUAL joint contribution of money, property, or industry are owned in common, and mere household care does not confer co-ownership.
C. Yes, under the principle of unjust enrichment in Article 22 of the Civil Code.
D. No, because the house and lot automatically belong 100% to Sofia as her exclusive paraphernal property.
⚡ 5-SEC RULE: Capacitated partners (Art. 147) = Equal 50/50 share (household care counts!). Incapacitated/Bigamous partners (Art. 148) = Must prove ACTUAL financial contribution (household care = P0)!
✅ Correct Answer: B (Arts. 147 & 148, Family Code; Agapay v. Palang; Ventura v. Spouses Abuda; Aquino, Reviewer on Civil Law (3rd Ed.))
1. Answer: No, Lorna's claim is not valid.
2. Legal Basis: Under Article 148 of the Family Code, in cases of cohabitation where the parties are incapacitated to marry each other (such as when one party is validly married to another), only the properties acquired by both of the parties through their actual joint contribution of money, property, or industry shall be owned by them in common in proportion to their respective contributions.
3. Application: Unlike Article 147, Article 148 does not credit the care and maintenance of the household as a contribution. Since the Quezon City property was bought solely from Dante's salary and Lorna made no actual financial contribution, Lorna acquired no co-ownership share, and Dante's share accrues to his conjugal/community property with his legal wife Sofia.
4. Conclusion: Therefore, Lorna gets nothing from the Quezon City house and lot.

B. Pedro, because while Mang Tomas automatically acquired ownership of the alluvial deposit by operation of law under Article 457 of the Civil Code, an unregistered alluvial deposit does NOT automatically become registered land under the Torrens system and is therefore subject to acquisition by prescription by third persons.
C. The State, because all riverbanks are property of public dominion.
D. Mang Tomas, unless Pedro paid him compensation within 2 years under Article 459.

✅ Correct Answer: B (Art. 457, Civil Code; Grande v. Court of Appeals (G.R. No. L-17652); Reynante v. CA; Aquino, Reviewer on Civil Law (3rd Ed.))
1. Answer: Pedro now owns the 2,000-sq.m. alluvial deposit by acquisitive prescription.
2. Legal Basis: Under Article 457 of the Civil Code, to the owners of lands adjoining the banks of rivers belongs the accretion which they gradually receive from the effects of the current of the waters. Court of Appeals, the Supreme Court held that an alluvial deposit does not automatically become registered land under the Torrens title of the riparian lot, and is therefore subject to acquisition through prescription by third persons if left unregistered.
3. Application: Although Mang Tomas initially owned the accretion by law, he failed to register it, allowing Pedro to possess it openly, continuously, and adversely for 35 years (exceeding the 30-year period for extraordinary prescription under Art. 1137).
4. Conclusion: Thus, Pedro acquired ownership of the unregistered alluvial strip by prescription.
